Voice Arena — Speaker Diarization Benchmark

Executive 1-Page Summary • Architecture, Rules & Key Findings
Author: Ansh Agnihotri
Target Audience: Product & Engineering Leaders
Time to Read: 90 Seconds
The Problem: Every speech AI vendor claims "99% accuracy" in marketing demos. But in real life, when people talk over each other on Zoom or speak on an 8 kHz phone line, those models break down. Voice Arena provides an independent, honest scorecard that tells product teams which model actually fits their specific application.

1. Who Uses This Leaderboard (3 Core Buyers)

Zoom / Notion AI Meeting Bots

Environment: 3–8 people, laptop mics, heavy interruptions and cross-talk.

Their Question: Which model correctly tracks action items when people interrupt each other?

Contact Centers (Gong / Talkdesk)

Environment: 2 people on 8 kHz phone lines (Agent vs. Customer).

Their Question: Which model guarantees zero speaker-flipping with sub-300ms speed?

Medical Scribes (Abridge / Epic)

Environment: Doctor, patient, and nurse; far-field room mic.

Their Question: Which model can run on-premise (HIPAA) with zero missed patient speech?

2. The 3 Ground Rules (How We Keep It Honest)

Rule Our Standard Why It Matters in Real Life
1. Collar Tolerance (Grace Period) 0ms Default (No Grace Period) Older tests give models a 250ms buffer, forgiving timing delays. In real products, a quarter-second delay makes voice bots feel laggy and interrupt people.
2. Overlapping Speech Fully Scored In real meetings, 10–15% of conversation overlaps. Ignoring cross-talk hides the #1 failure mode of speech AI.
3. Speaker Count Prior Blind Test (k Unknown) We never cheat by telling the AI how many people are speaking. The AI must figure it out on its own, just like in production.

3. Test Audio & Answer Key Pipeline

We test on a 50-Hour Curated Test Set across 4 domains: Team Meetings (20h), Phone Calls (15h), Healthcare (10h), and Debates (5h). To build the 100% correct answer key, human annotators mark speech boundaries in Label Studio, software (Montreal Forced Aligner) snaps boundaries to exact acoustic sound waves, and an inter-annotator quality gate (Cohen's Kappa > 0.92) ensures accuracy.

The Big Takeaway: There is no single best model for everything. pyannote 3.1 dominates multi-speaker team meetings with heavy cross-talk (3x better on overlap), while Deepgram Nova-3 is the undisputed champion for 2-person phone calls and live streaming speed.
