1. Purpose: Who This Benchmark Is For & The Decisions They Make

Speaker Diarization answers a deceptively simple question: "Who spoke when?" in an audio recording. While Speech-to-Text converts audio into words, diarization assigns each word to a speaker label (e.g., Speaker 0 vs. Speaker 1) without knowing who the people are or how many there are in advance.

Today, every voice vendor claims "99% accuracy" in their marketing. But in the real world—when people interrupt each other, use AirPods in a noisy cafe, or speak over a scratchy 8 kHz phone line—those systems fail. Voice Arena is an independent public leaderboard that helps engineering teams choose the right model based on verified, real-world data.

The 4 Core Buyer Personas

Buyer Persona Acoustic Environment The Critical Business Decision Cost of Model Failure

(Notion AI, Zoom, Otter)		 3 to 8 people; laptop mics and AirPods; constant interruptions, laughter, and keyboard noise. (Descript, Rev, Court Scribes)		 Courtrooms, debates, podcasts; exact word-level audio editing and legal attribution. Which model provides sub-100ms cut accuracy so automated audio cuts don't slice off words? Mutilated podcast cuts and legally disputed court deposition transcripts.

Solving the Fairness Challenge Across Different Architectures

Comparing a standalone acoustic interval engine (like pyannote) against a full speech-to-text API (like Deepgram) is an apples-to-oranges challenge. We enforce four strict fairness rules:

1. 300ms Pause-Bridging Rule

ASR APIs output word timestamps with blank spaces between words. Standalone diarizers output continuous speech blocks. To level the playing field, we bridge consecutive same-speaker words separated by <300ms into a continuous RTTM segment.

2. Strict Blind Diarization ($k = \text{Unknown}$)

Many commercial APIs allow passing num_speakers=2. Giving the AI a speaker count in advance artificially deflates errors by 30–50%. We evaluate all systems blindly without hints.

3. Identical Audio Input

All models receive bit-for-bit identical 16-bit PCM WAV audio. Phone tests are downsampled to 8 kHz mono prior to testing to replicate true telecom trunks.

4. Standardized Hardware Baseline

All self-hosted models are benchmarked on fixed cloud hardware (AWS g5.xlarge with NVIDIA A10G 24GB VRAM) to make latency and cost calculations reproducible.

Building the 100% Correct Answer Key (Reference Pipeline)

A benchmark is only as good as its answer key. We follow a 4-step curation protocol to guarantee human annotations are accurate down to the millisecond:

Stage 1 Dual Human Labeling: Two independent annotators mark speech boundaries and speaker IDs in Label Studio.
Stage 2 Forced Alignment: Montreal Forced Aligner (MFA) snaps boundaries to exact physical acoustic plosives and vowels.
Stage 3 Quality Gate: Inter-Annotator Agreement (Cohen's Kappa) must exceed 0.92. Any discrepancy >100ms is flagged.
Stage 4 Adjudication: A senior speech scientist resolves flagged segments to finalize the Golden Reference files.

4. Scoring Metrics & Ground Rules

1. Primary Metric: Diarization Error Rate (DER)

DER measures the percentage of speech time that the AI got wrong. It is the sum of three simple mistake types:

DER = (Missed Speech + False Alarm + Wrong Speaker) / Total Ground-Truth Speech Time

• Missed Speech: A person was talking, but the AI output silence.
• False Alarm: Nobody was talking (breath or noise), but the AI claimed someone spoke.
• Wrong Speaker: Someone spoke, but the AI attributed it to the wrong person.

2. The 3 Crucial Ground Rules

Scoring Setting Our Standard Why It Matters to Product Teams
Collar Tolerance (Grace Period) 0ms Default (No Grace Period) Academic tests give a 250ms buffer, forgiving late starts. But in real-time voice agents, a 250ms delay makes the AI interrupt people awkwardly. 0ms tests true boundary precision.
Overlapping Speech Included & Scored Separately Cross-talk represents 10–15% of business meetings. Excluding overlap hides where commercial models fail most severely.
Speaker Count Knowledge Blind ($k = \text{Unknown}$) In production, software never knows speaker counts beforehand. Blind testing tests true clustering ability.

6. Statistical Verification & Avoiding Lucky Winners

  • 10,000 Bootstrap Resamplings: We resample sessions with replacement 10,000 times to calculate empirical 95% Confidence Intervals for every score.
  • Paired Permutation Tests: A model is only declared superior to another if $p < 0.01$. If intervals overlap, the leaderboard flags them as statistically tied.
  • Published Metadata: Beside every score, we publish the 95% CI, Overlap DER, Real-Time Factor (RTF), cost per 1k hours, and exact model version.

7. Final Reflection (Assessment Questions)

1. Which design choice are you least sure about, and why?

The 300ms pause-bridging threshold for speech-to-text models.
When converting word timestamps from ASR models into continuous speech blocks, we bridge pauses under 300ms. If the threshold is too small (e.g., 100ms), natural breathing breaks look like false alarms. If it's too large (e.g., 500ms), it absorbs short confirmations ("yeah") from other speakers. While 300ms aligns with speech science literature, conversational tempo varies across cultures.

2. Which benchmark you read influenced you most, and how?

The Third DIHARD Diarization Challenge (Ryant et al., 2020).
DIHARD III proved that models that look great on clean academic audio (like AMI) completely collapse in the real world. It pioneered uncollared evaluation (0ms) and proved why blind speaker estimation is essential. We took DIHARD's clinical rigor and applied it to high-value business domains: Zoom meetings, phone calls, and clinical scribes.

3. What could make your leaderboard misleading, and how did you guard against it?

The "Single Average Score" Trap.
Our pilot proved that Deepgram dominates phone calls, but pyannote dominates team meetings. A single composite average score would mislead a buyer into picking the wrong tool. We guard against this by providing Domain-Specific Leaderboard Views and multi-dimensional radar profiles.

4. What would you improve with more time?

1. Network-Jitter Simulation: Testing streaming APIs under real-world packet loss to see how internet drops affect diarization.
2. Multi-Lingual Code-Switching: Testing bilingual conversations where speakers switch between languages mid-sentence (e.g., Hinglish or Spanglish).
3. Downstream LLM Impact: Measuring how diarization errors directly translate into wrong meeting summaries and corrupted action items.
