1. Purpose & Strategic Target Audience

Speaker Diarization answers "Who spoke when?" in conversational audio without prior voice enrollment or knowledge of speaker identities. While transcription converts speech to text, diarization establishes turn attribution, conversational dynamics, and accountability.

Current commercial marketing is plagued by unverified claims of "99% diarization accuracy" evaluated on pristine, synthetic audio. When deployed in production environments—characterized by rapid cross-talk, acoustic room reverberation, soft whispers, and packet loss—these systems suffer catastrophic degradation. The Voice Arena Leaderboard is designed as an independent, multi-dimensional decision engine that enables enterprise engineering and product leaders to answer:

Core Strategic Mission
Which diarization system truly delivers the required accuracy, latency, and cost profile for our specific acoustic domain, where does each system fail, and how do we make an evidence-based build vs. buy choice?

Standardizing Fairness Across Heterogeneous Architectures

Evaluating standalone acoustic interval diarizers alongside speech-to-text APIs creates severe apples-to-oranges risks. Voice Arena enforces four non-negotiable normalization rules:

1. Dual-Track Normalization Protocol

Acoustic Interval Track: For word-level ASR APIs (Deepgram, AssemblyAI), adjacent words assigned to the same speaker are merged into continuous RTTM segments if the intervening pause is <300ms.

Word Attribution Track: For acoustic diarizers (pyannote), a frozen baseline transcript (Whisper Large-v3 + forced alignment) is projected onto predicted speaker intervals to score word-level attribution.

2. Strict Blind Diarization ($k = \text{Unknown}$)

In production, systems are never informed whether a meeting has 3 or 7 speakers. Allowing systems to accept min_speakers or max_speakers artificially deflates error rates by 30–50%.

Rule: All primary leaderboard rankings strictly evaluate blind speaker clustering without oracle hints.

3. Uniform Audio Ingestion & Codec Standard

All models receive bit-for-bit identical 16-bit PCM WAV audio. Telephony benchmarks are systematically downsampled to 8 kHz mono prior to API ingestion to replicate actual telecom trunks.

4. Standardized Hardware & Latency Accounting

Self-hosted models run on fixed hardware (AWS g5.xlarge with NVIDIA A10G, 24GB VRAM). We record Real-Time Factor (RTF = Processing Time / Audio Duration) and P95 latency.

To prevent data contamination (models memorizing public corpora), Voice Arena deploys a **50-Hour Curated Test Corpus** comprising 250 distinct recordings stratified across 4 realistic production domains:

Demographic & Dialect Diversity Protocol

The corpus enforces strict dialect stratification: 40% Standard American English, 20% British/Commonwealth, 20% Indian English, and 20% Non-native ESL speakers (European, East Asian, Hispanic accents). This guarantees systems are evaluated against real-world phonetic variability.

Rigorous Ground-Truth (Reference) Construction Pipeline

A benchmark is only as reliable as its ground-truth answer key. Human annotator error is the single biggest cause of noisy benchmark rankings. Voice Arena establishes a 4-stage reference curation protocol:

``` Stage 1: Dual Independent Human Annotation └── Two expert phonetic annotators independently mark word boundaries and speaker IDs in Label Studio. Stage 2: Forced Phoneme Alignment └── Automated Montreal Forced Aligner (MFA) locks boundary onsets/offsets to physical acoustic plosives & fricatives. Stage 3: Statistical Inter-Annotator Agreement (IAA) Quality Gate └── Cohen's Kappa must exceed 0.92. Any frame discrepancy > 100ms triggers mandatory human review. Stage 4: Senior Phonetician Adjudication └── Master phonetician reconciles flagged segments to generate finalized Golden Reference RTTMs. ```

4. Evaluation Metrics & Scoring Rules

1. Primary Metric: Diarization Error Rate (DER)

DER represents the fraction of ground-truth speech time that is incorrectly attributed. It is mathematically defined as:

DER = (T_miss + T_fa + T_spkerr) / T_total

Where:
• T_total = Total active duration of all ground-truth reference speech.
• T_miss = Missed Speech: Reference is active, but system outputs silence.
• T_fa = False Alarm: Reference is silent, but system predicts active speech.
• T_spkerr = Speaker Confusion: System predicts speech, but maps it to wrong speaker cluster.

2. Supporting Diagnostic Metrics

  • Jaccard Error Rate (JER): Evaluates speaker clustering with equal weight across all speakers, preventing a single dominant speaker who talks for 70% of a call from masking poor clustering on minority speakers:
    JER = 1 - (1 / |S_ref|) * Σ [ max_(s' in S_hyp) ( |T(s) ∩ T(s')| / |T(s) ∪ T(s')| ) ]
  • Word Flop Error Rate (WFER): The percentage of transcribed words assigned to the wrong speaker. Directly actionable for product teams building automated meeting summaries and legal transcripts.
  • Overlap Diarization Error Rate (Overlap-DER): DER calculated exclusively on audio frames where two or more speakers are talking concurrently.
  • Operational Metrics: Real-Time Factor (RTF), Streaming P95 Latency (ms), and Cost per Audio Hour ($/hr).

Pivotal Scoring Rule Choices & Engineering Justification

Scoring Setting Benchmark Setting Engineering & Product Rationale
Collar Tolerance Window Dual Reporting: 0ms (Default) & 250ms (Toggle) Academic challenges (DIHARD, AMI) apply a 250ms collar to forgive human boundary variance. However, conversational voice agents and live meeting summaries fail if turn boundaries are off by 250ms. Voice Arena makes 0ms collar the default ranker to evaluate boundary precision, while providing 250ms for backwards literature compatibility.
Overlapping Speech Included & Scored Excluding overlapping speech hides the primary point of failure for voice AI products. Cross-talk represents 10–15% of business meetings; scoring overlap is mandatory for production validity.
Speaker Count Prior ($k$) Blind Mode ($k = \text{Unknown}$) In commercial software, the system does not know speaker counts in advance. Blind estimation tests the clustering algorithm's true capability.

6. Statistical Significance & Publishing Standards

To ensure enterprise buyers do not make purchasing decisions based on random statistical noise, Voice Arena implements rigorous verification:

  • Non-Parametric Bootstrap Resampling: We resample the evaluation sessions with replacement 10,000 times to construct empirical 95% Confidence Intervals for every score.
  • Paired Permutation Tests: A system is only declared superior to another if the paired permutation test yields $p < 0.01$. If the confidence intervals overlap, the leaderboard flags the systems as statistically tied.
  • Published Metadata Beside Each Score: 1. 95% CI Range (e.g., $3.07\% \pm 0.4\%$).
    2. Overlap DER vs Non-overlap DER.
    3. Hardware spec & Real-Time Factor (RTF).
    4. Exact dollar cost per 1,000 audio hours.
    5. Frozen model checksum and API endpoint version.

7. Final Reflection (Assessment Questions)

1. Which design choice are you least sure about, and why?

The 300ms inter-word pause bridging threshold for ASR-integrated models.
When converting word-level speaker tags into continuous RTTM segments, we bridge same-speaker pauses <300ms. If the threshold is too small (e.g., 100ms), natural breathing silences create artificial fragmented false alarms. If it is too large (e.g., 500ms), it absorbs short backchannel affirmations ("mm-hmm") from other speakers. While 300ms matches empirical phonetic turn-taking literature, human speech tempo varies across cultures.

2. Which benchmark you read influenced you most, and how?

The Third DIHARD Diarization Challenge (Ryant et al., 2020).
DIHARD III fundamentally dismantled the illusion created by clean datasets like AMI. It demonstrated that when audio moves to challenging acoustic spaces, single-channel diarization errors skyrocket from 3% to over 20%. DIHARD pioneered uncollared evaluation and isolated Overlap-DER. Voice Arena takes DIHARD's clinical rigor and redirects it from YouTube clips toward high-value enterprise workflows (telephony, clinical scribing, and enterprise meetings).

3. What could make your leaderboard misleading, and how did you guard against it?

Two risks: The "Global Average Fallacy" and "Test Set Contamination".
1. Global Average Fallacy: Our empirical pilot proved that Deepgram defeats pyannote on 8 kHz telephony, but pyannote dominates Deepgram by 3x on multi-speaker meeting overlap. A single composite score would mislead buyers. We guard against this by providing Persona-Specific Leaderboard Views and radar profiles.
2. Contamination: Public corpora (VoxConverse, AMI) are frequently included in commercial training sets. We guard against contamination by locking a Secret Golden Evaluation Split of newly recorded, unreleased sessions.

4. What would you improve with more time?

1. Network-Jitter Emulation for Streaming APIs: Simulating real-world WebRTC packet loss, burst drops, and jitter to observe real-time diarization degradation.
2. Multi-Lingual Code-Switching Slice: Curating conversations where bilingual speakers alternate languages mid-sentence (e.g., Spanglish or Hinglish), which frequently causes embedding models to split one person into two personas.
3. Downstream LLM Hallucination Impact: Measuring how diarization errors directly translate into corrupted meeting minutes (e.g., "Hallucinated Action Items per Meeting").
